# Literature ledger

Entries were read from the cited paper sources. The alphaXiv reader was unavailable for the second round, so the added entries were read through the arXiv HTML and abstract endpoints and a web reader; the verification standard is unchanged. The ledger records comparison boundaries rather than title-level similarity.

| Paper | Verified overlap | Boundary for this study |
|---|---|---|
| ReAct, arXiv:2210.03629, ICLR 2023 | Interleaves reasoning and acting and evaluates ALFWorld success. | It studies the actor scaffold, not outcome-based runtime intervention selection. |
| ScienceWorld, arXiv:2203.07540 | Defines the ScienceWorld task families, variations, and agent evaluation setting. | The present ScienceWorld panel is a boundary analysis with official-test variations and no retuning. |
| Reflexion, arXiv:2303.11366 | Uses verbal feedback from previous attempts to improve later attempts. | It does not estimate a one-step intervention menu from matched stochastic continuations. |
| Self-Refine, arXiv:2303.17651 | Iteratively generates feedback and refines outputs. | It does not isolate the value of a frozen runtime message relative to an explicit continuation arm. |
| Meta-learners for HTE, arXiv:1706.03461 | Separates response-function learners from learners that target conditional treatment effects. It reports that no meta-learner is uniformly best. | It motivates the feature- and capacity-matched outcome-versus-signed-benefit comparison; it does not itself validate causal language for agent rollouts. |
| Calibration Is Not Control, arXiv:2606.21399 | Defines intervention advantage and uses same-prefix branching. Its own page states a single-rollout branching approach per action, and the paper reports main controller regret from one realized branch per action. Appendix B.7 repeats three actions twice on 24 shallow prefixes and reports zero within-action variation there. | It leaves deeper prefixes under single-rollout evaluation. The present study freezes two fresh draws for all four actions on 441 planned tasks and separates action selection from evaluation draws. |
| COTA, arXiv:2608.21027 | Trains a 0.5B pairwise advisor and reports 82.84% to 90.30% ALFWorld success for Qwen3-8B. ALFWorld uses 134 valid-unseen tasks, a 30-step limit, four candidates, environment-action support, and 31.15 H200-hours of offline preparation. | Its actor protocol, action support, repeated online intervention, objective, and compute differ. It is a strong external reference, not the feature- and interface-matched comparator in this study. |
| DreamGuard, arXiv:2608.05695 | Learns a risk-aware recurrent world model and maps immediate and accumulated risk scores to `PASS`, `HOLD`, or `BLOCK`; its online evaluation reports a safety-utility trade-off and per-call latency. | Its decision is whether to permit a proposed action under a safety objective. It does not supply the matched utility-repair action menu studied here. |
| Harness-R1, arXiv:2608.02276 | Trains a separate engineer to edit four lifecycle hooks of a frozen agent. Candidate patches are scored by fresh same-batch reruns; the paper also evaluates patches inferred from ten failures on 1,270 held-out tasks across three seeds. | Its unit is a batch-conditioned executable overlay installed before rerunning tasks, rather than a per-prefix choice among fixed interventions. The held-out experiment reinforces the need to distinguish evidence tasks from evaluation tasks. |
| Toward a Principled Framework for Agent Safety Measurement, arXiv:2605.01644 | Argues that a greedy or small sampled set can conflate the realized trajectory with the deployment distribution; BOA searches the in-budget stochastic trajectory tree and probability-weights the resulting safety score. | Its target is tail safety under a likelihood budget, not treatment choice or independent evaluation of an outcome-selected intervention. It strengthens the distributional motivation but is not a controller comparator. |
| Recoverability-Aware Rollout Intervention Learning, arXiv:2608.05080 | RAIL learns an online contextual-bandit controller for where and how much to branch during post-training. It includes a no-branch utility gate and targets improvement in rollout reward contrast. | Its intervention changes training-data allocation for an evolving policy. The present estimand is terminal task success for a frozen actor under a fixed message menu, so RAIL is adjacent rather than matched. |
| COTA, arXiv:2608.21027 | Trains a 0.5B pairwise advisor. Its supervision is built from same-prefix counterfactual branches and labelled by comparing Monte Carlo branch returns, which is the construction whose reuse this paper measures. Reports 82.84% to 90.30% ALFWorld success for Qwen3-8B under a 30-step actor with four environment-action candidates and repeated online intervention. | Its actor protocol, action support, intervention schedule, objective, and compute differ. We instead run the comparison-only objective its gate implies inside our frozen interface, matched on features, folds, capacity, and decision budget. |
| Asymmetric actor-critic, arXiv:2604.00304 | Pairs a large frozen actor with a small critic that intervenes inside the same trajectory and reports gains on tau-bench and UserBench. | The critic produces free-form corrections; it does not estimate the utility of declining to intervene, and its action support is unrestricted. |
| AgentTether, arXiv:2607.06273 | Abstracts a run into transition units, localizes a failure-critical subtrajectory on a dependency graph, and re-executes with scoped guidance; repairs 59.04% of failed Qwen3.7-max Banking tasks. | Its unit is a post-run repair with re-execution, not a per-prefix choice among fixed repairs with a continuation arm. |
| LATS, arXiv:2310.04406, ICML 2024 | Runs Monte Carlo tree search over language-agent continuations with an LM value function and commits to the best-scoring branch. | It is the deployable form of the outcome-informed selector in Equation 2 and spends many suffixes per decision; our menu spends one, so we report the selector as a diagnostic rather than as a matched baseline. |
| tau-bench, arXiv:2406.12045 | Introduces tool-agent-user domains with a simulated user and the pass^k reliability metric over independent trials. | Its randomness includes tools and the simulated user, so continuation variance is higher than in our environments; by the decomposition in Appendix A this raises the same-draw gap, making our estimate a lower reference for such domains. |
| Adding Error Bars to Evals, arXiv:2411.00640 | Treats evaluation items as a sample from a super-population, recommends paired comparisons, and names resampling of answers as the variance reduction that raises power. | It addresses single-turn evaluations; the repeated-branch protocol here is that variance reduction applied to agent rollouts, and its sample-size framework is what sizes the confirmation panel. |
| The reusable holdout, arXiv:1506.02629 | Formalizes overfitting caused by adaptive reuse of holdout data. | It motivates a new identity-disjoint panel but does not directly supply an inference guarantee for this clustered agent benchmark. |

The source-structure audit in `artifacts/reference-style/` uses ReAct, ScienceWorld, Reflexion, and Self-Refine as pre-2024 examples. It records section lengths and float conventions only. Perplexity targeting and author imitation are excluded.
