Selecting an intervention after observing stochastic branch outcomes, then scoring it on those outcomes, credits favorable continuation noise. We test this problem with four actions, including no intervention, and two independent continuations from each shared prefix. The confirmation covers 384 identity-disjoint ALFWorld tasks (96 floorplan groups), with predictions frozen before arm outcomes. The same-draw maximum exceeds cross-draw evaluation by 15.36 percentage points on the planned-task panel (group-bootstrap 95\% interval [12.46, 18.30]). The two-draw Direct and Arm outcome forests share features, folds, capacity, and action support. In the primary policy comparisons, Direct changes success by 1.56 points versus continuation and -0.39 points versus the strongest runnable matched controller. Neither controller contrast is significant, with or without multiplicity correction. The gap diagnoses outcome reuse, not bias in a precommitted policy. We retain the complete ScienceWorld boundary panel and adverse historical results. The protocol separates outcome-selected rescues from the value of prefix-only policies.
