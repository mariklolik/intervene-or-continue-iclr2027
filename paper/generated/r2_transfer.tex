We can also move a controller to where the room is. We fitted the frozen rule on development records collected under the triggered rule and then read it under both, which is the decision-point mismatch that Section~\ref{sec:estimand} warns about. We therefore refit the same learner on development records collected under the scheduled rule, and we break grid ties by how often the nested inner folds made the same choice rather than by the firing rate, which is the selection-frequency criterion of \citet{meinshausen2010stability}. That raises scheduled-rule success from 55.23\% to 58.26\% and puts cross-draw control ahead of every comparator on that panel as well: 3.54 points over continuation (adjusted $p=0.0073$), 0.34 over the failure-risk detector, 0.17 over the repeated arm-outcome forest, and 0.76 over the best fixed repair. The rule it learns is the unconditional repair on 472 of 540 eligible prefixes together with a learned decline on 64: at a scheduled step the prefix says when not to repair rather than which repair to use. The fit post-dates that panel's outcomes, so this reading is reported beside the frozen confirmation and not in place of it.

Two variants did not transfer, and we report both. Carrying the same static fallback to the triggered decision point moves success from 56.24\% to 55.73\%, which the interval of the contrast separating the two rules already contains. Gating the fallback on the development room reading inverts it. The development panels read $-$0.29 and 1.03 points where the confirmation panels read 1.71 and $-$0.83. At a few hundred tasks we therefore read the room on the panel we will deploy on rather than carry it from development.
